Reflection

What did you decide to map and why?

We decided to map the Snell Library Quad located just outside the Curry Student Center as we realised that it gets the most foot traffic, whether people are commuting between home and campus using the T or visiting the library between classes. We expected this area to be where people would be mostly walking by at busy times of the day. This space offers a natural setting to observe different behaviour. We also figured that when people are walking, they usually listen to music at that time and aren’t focused on work or class at that moment as compared to if they were sitting down and working. This could suggest that emotions are more easily identifiable and range across different categories. We specifically focused on the songs people were listening to and their moods/emotions because we wanted to explore the relationship between music and emotion in a public setting.

Whose story are you telling with your map and whose story are you not telling?

The story we are telling are the students of Northeastern and their daily routine based on their mood as expressed through their music choices on contrasting days. We picked two different days (Friday and Sunday) that we thought would have the biggest contrast. Friday (Halloween) is a day to celebrate finishing the week and getting ready for the exciting weekend, while Sunday is focused on resetting for the upcoming week and is considered a day of rest and relaxation. This essentially captures how students' emotional states and music preferences shift between a festive evening and a quiet weekend morning. It also depicts the relationship between specific genres and emotional feelings. The Snell Quad is located on Northeastern University Campus grounds, and so the range of people does not reach the general population, like those going to work, people going to school, etc. It is limited to primarily Northeastern students. This map also excludes people who aren’t listening to music or weren’t comfortable with sharing their song choices with us.

What problems and opportunities does looking at the NU campus/neighborhood/Boston through your map reveal to us?

Looking at the Northeastern University campus and neighborhood through our map can reveal certain problems relating to students every-day lives highlighting the connection between mood, music, and time. Every student has their own fixed schedule and routine, and the genre of music they are listening to at different times of the day can demonstrate their emotional and mental state; such as, on a Monday morning the student can feel tired so maybe they will listen to rap music to hype them up and give them more energy for the day. Or if they are feeling a little down and so they listen to a somber tune. Mapping out the Snell Library Quad through music genre and mood can help unveil a pattern in Northeastern campus life and reflect a certain consensus that all students share in their hardships and community. Our map can also indicate how the environment can affect mood by showing how different areas of the Snell Library Quad could inflict different types of music based on a person's surroundings.

Part 2: Research and Proposal

I. Abstract

Our proposed idea explores how an intervention can reveal individuals’ emotions in mobile environments. Using the Snell Library Quad as our intervention space, we developed an instruction set where people can express their emotions nonverbally through color and participation. The proposal outlines our site research, the overall concept, inspirations from past examples and suggested artists, and the intended interaction.

II. Extended Site Research

After finishing our mapping submission, we went back to Snell Library Quad to conduct further research on the space. We went back at different times to understand how the space functions at various times of the day. One thing we noticed across almost all times is that people do not tend to linger in the area, but instead just pass through. Almost everyone is in transit, whether heading to class or to grab food in Curry. People also tend to move in a directional flow, following the same paths that are naturally created. The only place to pause, socialize, or look around is towards the sides where people use the ledges to sit, often in between classes. However, the quad behaves more like a circulatory system than a gathering space.

We also noted how the temperature affected moods and behaviours while walking through the space. For example, when it is colder out, people walk faster and appear less aware of those around them (more driven towards getting to their destination). There also seemed to be less traffic when it’s colder, possibly due to a variety of factors like people intentionally skipping their activities for the day or utilizing the underground tunnels. When it is warmer, more people are involved in conversations with friends as they walk.

Based on this research, we were able to conclude that our intervention must be quick, intuitive, and easy to understand at a glance. Requiring individuals to participate in an activity with complex instructions of a time-consuming nature would not be as effective. The design has to be something they can understand instantly and participate in with minimal interruption to their day. This insight led us to develop the Wishing Well, where participants can express their moods through visual cues in a timely manner.

III. Intervention Proposal

For this intervention, we chose to locate it on the Snell Library Quad because there is enough foot traffic there to create an effect. Building on our Mapping an Experience task, we decided to use this intervention to visualize the different emotions people are feeling as they walk past and to investigate what emotions are most commonly felt during different times of the week. Beyond capturing people's moods, we will be extending positive messages to all participants to give kindness in order to receive kindness.

To visualize this idea, we are planning to create a large sign inviting people to participate through texts and visual appeal. This sign will highlight our key message of "give to take," along with a direct cue of "Tell us your mood!" to deliver the aim of our intervention. Once people are interested, they will be able to pick up a colored paper coin and drop it into our wishing well. All participants will be given a choice between coins of six different colors. Each color of the coins will represent a different emotion: red for feeling powerful, grey for fatigue, yellow for happy, orange for energetic, pink for romantic, and blue for sad. After making a choice corresponding to their current mood, they will drop it into a wishing well, which documents all the participants' emotions.

We want our intervention to be more interactive with the participants themselves and to limit the interference of team members in the experience. To do this, we want to make the process of picking the coins more intuitive by drawing different emojis on each colored coin. For example, the yellow coin will have a smiley face on one side, and the blue coin will have a sad face drawn on it. In case that is not clear enough for people, we will also have a visual key next to the coins, annotating the emotion for each color.

By the end of each session, our team members will count and document the number of coins in the wishing well along with their colors. This will tell us the most popular moods of people passing by the Snell Library Quad during our session.

IV. Class Example Inspiration

A large part of our idea was inspired by the many examples we have seen in class. One of examples was the Mood Matrix and their way of collecting data. What stood out to us in its design was the utilization of bright and alluring colors, and the simple drawing of faces that represent the range of emotions people might feel during that moment on the Brooklyn Bridge. The use of color stood out as an important component to representing how people feel, and taking that idea and making it into an interactive experience was a core component into coming up with an idea of how to create something interactive in the Snell Library Quad that will help connect the community. The idea of making the colors into different coins passersby can use comes from another example that was shown in class which was the Dove Campaign For Real Beauty. What stood out was how something as simple as choosing which door to go through based on what it was labeled (beautiful or average), told a lot about how women saw themselves and how there is a general consensus around the world that women have where they think they are average which says a lot about what the definition of beauty means to society. The fact that this campaign did not interrupt anyone’s daily routine in a significant way is something that we also want to incorporate within our project. This is due to the fact that many people who pass by the Library Quad are students from Northeastern and many are on their way to class, so their time is precious. This is why we referred to the Dove campaign and came up with the idea of having pathways so that people can simultaneously interact with the project and go on their way.

V. Related Works by Artists and Designers

Our project's focus on nonverbal emotional expression and subtle interaction through participation connects strongly to the work of Hélio Oiticica and Olafur Eliasson, two artists who use color, environment, and bodily participation to guide audience behavior without direct instruction.

Hélio Oiticica is known for creating immersive environments that transform viewers into active participants. His "Parangolés" and other large-scale installations rely on color, texture, and spatial composition as nonverbal cues that encourage people to walk, explore, or gesture intuitively. Oiticica treats color not just as a visual component but as a sensory and emotional experience that shifts mood and perception. This relates directly to our intervention on the Snell Library Quad, where each coin thrown into the wishing well uses color as an immediate emotional signifier. Like Oiticica's work, our design is not based upon written instruction; the presence of colored coins and expressive emojis acts as a visual prompt intuitively guiding people's actions in the experience. His focus on bodily navigation through color-rich environments supports our goal of integrating interaction seamlessly into a mobile, high-traffic context.

Olafur Eliasson similarly works with nonverbal communication through light, color, atmosphere, and environmental perception. In projects such as "The Weather Project," Eliasson creates situations where simple visual stimuli evoke individual emotional responses while simultaneously revealing collective patterns of behavior. His installations use subtle cues—rather than explicit directions—to heighten people's awareness of themselves, others, and the space they move through. Our colored coins function in a similar way: instead of verbally asking passersby to express their feelings, the colors and emoji-based symbols act as gentle invitations for emotional expression through dropping their emotional coins in a wishing well. Eliasson's interest in how environmental changes reflect collective behavior parallels our plan to analyze fading chalk over time, using it as a visual record of shared emotional states among people crossing the quad.

Part 3: Interventions

Project Overview
  • Intervention exploring how emotions can be revealed through simple, non-verbal participation
  • Location: Snell Library Quad
  • Uses colored "mood coins" + a wishing well to invite strangers to express emotions with minimal interruption
  • Design choices made based on site research + inspiration from previous works
Project Goals

QUICK & INTUITIVE PARTICIPATION Create an interaction that participants can understand instantly and participate in without overly disrupting their movement.

ENCOURAGE NONVERBALEMOTIONAL EXPRESSION Use colors, symbols, and gestures to feel connected to the collective mood of campus.

VISUALIZE MOOD PATTERNS Document and display the overall emotional states of participants, revealing patterns across a variety of variables.

Research Recap

Site Observations
  • Snell quad behaves as a circulatory space, not a gathering spot
  • People rarely stop, always in motion and follow a directional flow
  • Lingering can occur on the ledges that surround the quad

Insight: intervention should be fast, intuitive, and non-disruptive

Related Works
  • Mood Matrix: utilizes bright colors, simple facial drawings, showed how color can make emotional data understandable almost instantly
  • Dove Real Beauty Campaign: simple choices between two options, showed power of a small action to spark self reflection
Related Artists
  • Hélio Oiticica: uses color, texture, and space as nonverbal cues to guide movement + emotional response
  • Olafur Eliasson: uses subtle visual stimuli instead of text to encourage intuitive, personal responses

Inspirations: collecting emotional patterns through colored emotion coins and simple, environment-based participation with visual prompts that require limited written instructions

Ideation

IDEA 1: CHALK PATHWAYS
  • Six colored chalk paths representing emotions
  • Interact with visual cues (colored lines), emoji labels, intuitive engagement (minimal effort required to participate)

Why this didn't work: Hard to measure data/results, weather-dependent, lot of set-up effort required

IDEA 2: WISHING WELL
  • Colored mood coins (6 emotions) + wishing well for dropping coins
  • Simple interaction: select coin matching mood, drop in well
  • Sign explained concept, coin box for selection

Why we chose this: easier setup, quick participation + interaction, easily monitored

Goal: Visualize emotions people feel while walking through and identify most common emotions by time of week

Step 2: Intervention 1 Concept

Our intervention, Wishing Well, is designed for fast-moving environments like the Snell Library Quad, where people rarely stop but continually pass through. Based on our site research, the interaction focuses on being quick, intuitive, and visually communicative so participants can engage without disrupting their pace.

1. Invitation Sign
  • A large, visually engaging sign invites participation at a glance.
  • Highlights the project’s key message: “Give to take — tell us your mood!”
  • Uses color and simple graphics to communicate the action immediately.
2. Choose a Colored Coin

Participants select one of six color-coded emotional coins, each paired with a matching emoji to strengthen nonverbal recognition:

    ● Red – Powerful 💪
    ● Grey – Tired 😴
    ● Yellow – Happy 😊
    ● Orange – Energetic ⚡
    ● Pink – Romantic ❤️
    ● Blue – Sad 😢
3. Drop Into the Wishing Well
  • Participants drop their chosen coin into the wishing well as they walk by.
  • The action is fast and intuitive, requiring no verbal explanation.
  • The well passively collects emotional data across different times of day.
Why This Works for the Snell Quad
  • Designed for a high-traffic space where people move continuously.
  • Colors + emojis act as universal prompts—no need for complex text.
  • Creates a moment of self-reflection that fits seamlessly into daily movement.
  • Aligned with Dove’s “Real Beauty” campaign: simple choices → meaningful insights.

Step 3: Intervention 1 – Reflection & Insights

Results
  • ~40 interactions with the project
  • Some people were curious about the purpose of the project
Learnings
  • “Wishing Well” box and signs were too small to be noticed
  • People preferred short, simple interactions
Feedback Reflection

Our first intervention successfully engaged participants. However, it can be strengthened with deeper meaning, stronger visual presence, and more cohesive interaction design.

Next Steps

  • Clarify the message we want to deliver about “mood”
  • Explore delivery methods (verbal/textual vs. visual/physical)
  • Decide which elements to keep, redesign, or remove

Step 4: Intervention 2 – Concept

  • Key Message: “Give to Take”
  • Call-to-Action: “Tell us your mood!” — leave a message, take a message
How It Works:
  • 1. Write & Give
    • Participants write a positive message for someone else
    • Place their note into the message box
  • 2. Select & Drop
    • Choose a colored coin representing their current mood
    • Drop the coin into the wishing well
  • 3. Receive & Take
    • A team member shakes the message box to mix the messages
    • Participant takes a positive note written by someone else

Step 5: Revision & Reflection

Positive Things
  • Our intervention was light and inviting
  • Having a Wishing Well was intuitive
  • Using transparent material increased participation
Hardships & Obstacles
  • The wind was too strong
  • The well broke and slips flew away
  • Rush hour crowds made the box hard to see
  • Some students weren't back from break
Next Steps
  • Make the setup heavier and more wind-resistant
  • Run it on a later day once more students return
  • Choose a sunnier environment
  • Use a better camera for documentation
